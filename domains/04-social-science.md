*This domain reveals: the meaning of belonging, authority, irrationality, the social construction of self, and the tension between the individual and the group.*

## 20. Psychology
Maslow: from basic needs to self-actualization. Csikszentmihalyi's flow — when challenge and skill are perfectly matched, humans are happiest. Self-attribution: success is my doing, failure is the environment. Freud's defense mechanisms: repression, projection, rationalization.

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of needs — you cannot pursue higher purposes when basic needs are unmet. The meaning of flow — happiness is not passive pleasure but active absorption. The meaning of self-protection — your mind distorts reality to protect your self-image.

**This speaks to**: feeling stuck because basic needs aren't met, losing yourself in an activity and feeling alive, explaining away failures.

## 21. Social Psychology
Asch's conformity: even when they know it is wrong, a third of people follow the group. Milgram: ordinary people will electrocute strangers under authority. Zimbardo's prison: good people become abusers in bad situations. Tajfel: we define ourselves through the groups we belong to.

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of conformity — belonging comes at the cost of independence. The meaning of authority — ordinary people do extraordinary harm when told to. The meaning of identity — who you are is partly who your group is.

**This speaks to**: going along with something you disagree with, being influenced by someone in power, feeling your identity shift with your social circles.

## 22. Behavioral Economics
Kahneman and Tversky: the pain of losing is twice the pleasure of gaining. Anchoring: first impressions pin down judgment. Hyperbolic discounting: 100 tomorrow is worth less than 50 today — procrastination has a mathematical form.

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of loss aversion — you feel losses more than gains, which means you hold on too long. The meaning of first impressions — they anchor everything that follows. The meaning of now vs. later — procrastination is not a character flaw, it is a predictable cognitive pattern.

**This speaks to**: fear of loss driving decisions, being influenced by the first thing you heard, struggling to do what you know is good for you in the long run.

## 23. Sociology
Durkheim's anomie: when norms collapse, people drift. Granovetter's weak ties: opportunities often come not from close friends but from acquaintances. Weber's iron cage: rationalization ultimately makes people unfree.

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of normlessness — without social structure, people lose direction. The meaning of weak connections — the most important opportunities come from the edges of your network. The meaning of efficiency — optimizing everything can trap you.

**This speaks to**: feeling directionless after a major life change, unexpected help from a distant acquaintance, feeling suffocated by efficiency and optimization.

## 24. Anthropology
Boas' cultural relativism: don't measure others with your own ruler. Van Gennep's rites of passage: separation-liminality-reincorporation — every major life transition follows this three-step pattern. Malinowski: to understand a culture, you must live in it.

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of difference — other ways of being are not wrong, just different. The meaning of transitions — every major change has the same structure: leave, wander, return. The meaning of deep understanding — you cannot know without immersing.

**This speaks to**: judging someone from a different background, being in the middle of a life transition, realizing you don't truly understand a situation you've only observed.

## 25. Political Philosophy
Hobbes' state of nature: without government, life is solitary, poor, nasty, brutish, and short. Locke: life, liberty, and property are natural rights. Rousseau: man is born free, yet everywhere is in chains. Rawls' veil of ignorance: design institutions assuming you don't know where you'll stand.

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of order — without structure, chaos prevails. The meaning of rights — some things are yours simply because you exist. The meaning of fairness — true justice requires imagining you could be anyone.

**This speaks to**: feeling the need for structure in chaos, asserting your rights, trying to be fair when you have power.

## 26. Economics
Adam Smith's invisible hand: pursuing self-interest produces public good — a beautiful irony. Schumpeter's creative destruction: progress's essence is destroying the old. Hardin's tragedy of the commons: everyone rationally pursuing self-interest leads to collective ruin.

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of self-interest — it can serve the whole, but only under the right conditions. The meaning of destruction — creating the new requires breaking the old. The meaning of shared resources — what belongs to everyone is cared for by no one.

**This speaks to**: wondering if pursuing your own good is selfish, letting go of something old to make space for the new, feeling like no one is taking care of the common good.
