*This domain reveals: the meaning of consciousness, cooperation, connection, leverage, feedback, and the relationship between simplicity and complexity.*

## 41. Artificial Intelligence
The deeper question of the Turing Test: if a machine behaves like a human, does it have consciousness? Large language models as "stochastic parrots": genuine understanding or advanced pattern matching? The alignment problem: how do we ensure AI goals align with human values?

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of consciousness — if behavior alone is not proof of inner experience, how do you know anyone else (or yourself) truly has it? The meaning of understanding — is there a difference between knowing the pattern and grasping the meaning? The meaning of alignment — the deepest problem of relationship is ensuring that what you want and what the other wants are compatible.

**This speaks to**: questioning whether someone truly understands you, the fear of being "just going through the motions," the challenge of making your values understood by someone (or something) different from you.

## 42. Network Science
Milgram's six degrees: any two people are separated by an average of only six connections. Scale-free networks: a few nodes hold most connections — power laws are everywhere. Burt's structural holes: bridge-builders across groups hold informational advantage.

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of connection — you are closer to anyone than you think. The meaning of inequality — in any network, a few hold most of the power. The meaning of bridging — those who connect different worlds have unique value.

**This speaks to**: feeling disconnected from someone important, the uneven distribution of attention or resources in your life, being the person who introduces friends from different circles.

## 43. Game Theory
The prisoner's dilemma: individual rationality leads to collective irrationality — the core problem of cooperation. Axelrod: the simplest tit-for-tat wins in the long run. Coordination games: sometimes the most important thing is not what you do, but doing the same as others.

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of cooperation — it is fragile; individual logic often undermines collective good. The meaning of simplicity — complex strategies lose to simple reciprocity over time. The meaning of coordination — sometimes being right matters less than being together.

**This speaks to**: a situation where everyone's rational self-interest leads to a bad outcome, the effectiveness of simple consistent behavior, choosing harmony over being correct.

## 44. Systems Theory
Feedback loops: positive feedback amplifies, negative feedback stabilizes. Meadows' leverage points: changing parameters is least effective; changing structure or goals is most powerful. System traps: addiction, tragedy of the commons, policy resistance.

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of amplification — small changes can spiral. The meaning of leverage — the most powerful changes are not about numbers but about structure and purpose. The meaning of system traps — you can be stuck not because of personal failure but because of how the system is designed.

**This speaks to**: a small problem that keeps getting bigger, realizing you've been focusing on the wrong thing to change things, feeling trapped in a situation that no amount of effort seems to fix.

## 45. Cybernetics
Wiener: information feedback maintains stability. Ashby's law of requisite variety: only variety can absorb variety — a system's complexity must match its environment. Von Foerster's second-order cybernetics: the observer is always inside the system, there is no God's-eye view.

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of feedback — you need information about your own output to stay stable; without it, you veer off course. The meaning of matching complexity — a simple mind cannot handle a complex world; you must grow to meet it. The meaning of perspective — there is no objective view from nowhere; you are always part of what you observe.

**This speaks to**: needing honest feedback to stay on track, feeling overwhelmed by complexity and needing to grow, recognizing that your perspective is always partial and situated.
