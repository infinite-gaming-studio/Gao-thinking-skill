*This domain reveals: the meaning of impermanence, adaptation, uncertainty, the nature of reality, and the relationship between the observer and the observed.*

## 1. Physics
Heisenberg's observer effect: observing changes the observed. Newton vs. Bohr — is the world deterministic or probabilistic? The second law of thermodynamics: entropy always increases, the secret of time's arrow. Feynman said no one truly understands quantum mechanics — at the deepest level, reality may always have a veil.

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of uncertainty — not knowing is not a flaw, it is the fundamental nature of things. The meaning of time's direction — everything tends toward disorder, and this is why moments matter.

**This speaks to**: feeling uncertain about a decision, sensing that things are falling apart, wondering if there is a "right answer" out there.

## 2. Cosmology
Carl Sagan: we are all made of star dust. Hubble's expanding universe, dark energy's accelerating expansion. The Copernican principle recurs — we are not the center. Fermi's paradox: the universe is boundless, so where are the aliens? Silence itself is a kind of sound.

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of smallness — you are not the center, but you are made of the same stuff as stars. The meaning of silence — absence is not emptiness; the universe's silence asks a question.

**This speaks to**: feeling insignificant, wondering "does any of this matter?", the experience of solitude.

## 3. Biology & Evolution
Darwin: it is not the strongest who survive, but the most adaptable. The Red Queen — you must keep running just to stay in place. Convergent evolution: different species independently find similar solutions. Fitness is always relative, always contextual.

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of struggle — without environmental pressure, there is no growth. The meaning of "not good enough" — evolution says there is no "good enough," only "good enough for now." The meaning of competition and symbiosis — they are two sides of survival.

**This speaks to**: feeling inadequate, career or life transitions, the pressure to keep up, the question "why is this so hard."

## 4. Neuroscience
Brain plasticity: neural pathways are like forest trails — they widen with use. The default mode network — your brain keeps busy even when you rest, ruminating, wandering, remembering. Mirror neurons: you simulate others' actions in your mind. The hard problem of consciousness: how do electrical signals become feeling?

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of habit — every repetition carves a deeper groove. The meaning of rest — the brain is never off, it processes even when you stop. The meaning of empathy — you feel others because your brain simulates them.

**This speaks to**: being stuck in a pattern, feeling tired but unable to stop thinking, the mystery of why others' pain affects you.

## 5. Cognitive Science
Kahneman's System 1 and System 2: the fast one is intuition, the slow one is reason — but the slow one is lazy. Festinger's cognitive dissonance: when belief and action clash, facts lose. Lakoff's conceptual metaphors: we understand the abstract through bodily experience — "time is money" is not a metaphor, it is how we think about time.

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of self-deception — you are designed to protect your beliefs, not to be correct. The meaning of intuition — the fast system is not stupid, it is experienced. The meaning of abstract thinking — even the most ethereal thoughts are rooted in the body.

**This speaks to**: knowing you should change but not changing, feeling torn between gut feeling and logic, the strange power of a well-chosen metaphor.

## 6. Genetics & Epigenetics
Genes are not a verdict. Epigenetics shows the environment can flip genetic switches without rewriting the sequence. Lamarck was mocked for two centuries, now partially vindicated. Nature and nurture are not a binary — they are a counterpoint.

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of fate vs. freedom — your starting point is not your destination. The meaning of inheritance — you carry more than genes; you carry environment, history, and choices that flip switches.

**This speaks to**: feeling trapped by family history or biology, wondering if change is possible, the tension between "this is who I am" and "I can become otherwise."

## 7. Ecology
Ecological niche: every species has its place. The intermediate disturbance hypothesis: moderate disruption increases biodiversity. Symbiosis — lichen is a partnership of algae and fungi; your mitochondria were once independent bacteria.

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of belonging — every being has a place where it thrives. The meaning of disruption — not all disturbance is destruction; moderate chaos creates room for new growth.

**This speaks to**: not fitting in, experiencing disruption that feels destructive but might be generative, the discovery that you are made of relationships.

## 8. Complexity & Chaos
Lorenz's butterfly: one flap of wings in the Amazon can trigger a tornado in Texas two weeks later — not poetry, but differential equations. Emergence: each ant is dim, the colony is brilliant. Self-organized criticality: systems drift to the edge, and avalanche sizes follow a power law.

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of small actions — they amplify in ways you cannot trace. The meaning of the whole — you do not need to understand everything to be part of something intelligent.

**This speaks to**: wondering if a small decision matters, feeling like a small part of a large system, the surprising outcomes of simple beginnings.

## 9. Information Theory
Shannon: information is the reduction of uncertainty. Entropy measures both disorder and information — the same formula. Redundancy and noise in dialectic: without redundancy there is no error correction, but too much redundancy kills efficiency.

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of uncertainty — it is the raw material of information. The meaning of noise — what seems like interference may contain a signal. The meaning of repetition — redundancy is not useless; it is how fragile messages survive.

**This speaks to**: feeling confused (uncertainty as opportunity for learning), being overwhelmed by noise in life, the value of saying things more than once.

## 10. Statistics & Probability
Bayes: every new piece of evidence should update your beliefs. Regression to the mean: peaks are always followed by dips. Simpson's paradox: a trend in groups reverses when combined — data never lies, but questions can be asked wrong.

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of updating — wisdom is not holding firm but revising with evidence. The meaning of peaks and valleys — extremes are temporary; equilibrium returns. The meaning of perspective — how you frame the question changes the answer.

**This speaks to**: changing your mind after new information, feeling like you're in a slump after a high, realizing you might be looking at the problem wrong.
