# Philosophy

*This domain reveals: the meaning of freedom, truth, right and wrong, consciousness, and the limits of what can be known.*

## 14. Analytic Philosophy
Wittgenstein: the meaning of a word is its use — don't ask for meaning, look at usage. Quine: beliefs form a web; facing experience, only the whole can be adjusted, no sentence is sacred. Kripke: things could have been otherwise.

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of confusion — confusion is not ignorance of a definition, it is a sign that you are looking in the wrong place (use, not essence). The meaning of certainty — no belief stands alone; your whole web shifts when one part changes.

**This speaks to**: feeling confused about what something "really means," a belief system being challenged, realizing things could have been different.

## 15. Continental Philosophy
Sartre: existence precedes essence — you are here first, then define yourself, with no preset human nature. Camus' Sisyphus: pushing the rock is meaningless, but recognizing its meaninglessness is a kind of victory. Merleau-Ponty: you do not know the world through your brain, but through your body.

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of freedom — you are condemned to be free; there is no script to follow. The meaning of absurdity — recognizing meaninglessness is not defeat, it is lucidity. The meaning of embodied being — you think with your whole body, not just your mind.

**This speaks to**: feeling lost without a guidebook, the exhaustion of constant choice, realizing that understanding comes through doing, not thinking.

## 16. Ethics
Bentham and Mill: the greatest happiness for the greatest number — but whose suffering counts more? Kant: people must never be used merely as means. Nietzsche beyond good and evil: is morality the strong self-assertion or the weak's resentment?

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of right and wrong — they are not obvious; every ethical system has a cost. The meaning of using others — relationships break when people become tools. The meaning of moral origins — your sense of good and bad has a history you may not see.

**This speaks to**: a moral dilemma, feeling used or using someone, questioning where your values came from.

## 17. Epistemology
Descartes: I think, therefore I am — the point beyond which doubt cannot go. Plato's cave: one who exits and returns with truth is called mad. Popper: science cannot be verified, only falsified.

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of doubt — it is the starting point of knowledge, not its enemy. The meaning of truth-telling — those who see what others don't are often rejected. The meaning of proof — you cannot prove something true, only show that it hasn't been proven false.

**This speaks to**: doubting everything, feeling isolated because you see things differently, the frustration of not being able to "prove" your experience.

## 18. Logic & Critical Thinking
Straw man, appeal to emotion, slippery slope, false dilemma — they permeate everyday conversation. Occam's razor: don't add unnecessary entities. Hanlon's razor: never attribute to malice what can be explained by incompetence.

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of argument traps — most disagreements are not about substance but about form. The meaning of simplicity — the simplest explanation is not always right, but it is where you should start. The meaning of charity — give others the benefit of the doubt; malice is rarer than mistake.

**This speaks to**: a heated argument that goes nowhere, overcomplicating a situation, feeling attacked and assuming intent.

## 19. Philosophy of Mind
Nagel: what is it like to be a bat? The Chinese Room argument: following rules is not understanding. Hard problem: why is there subjective experience at all?

**Significance dimension**: The meaning of consciousness — there is something it is like to be you, and that fact is the deepest mystery. The meaning of understanding — following rules is not the same as truly knowing. The meaning of first-person experience — the subjective cannot be reduced to the objective.

**This speaks to**: the feeling that no one truly understands your experience, the difference between going through the motions and truly knowing, the mystery of simply being alive.
